Go1 PACT, Go1 ABL3, and Go1 PACT-Pos pretraining use the final physics substep of each policy transition for GRF learning. History is the policy input before the transition. The target is the unsmoothed endpoint foot force returned by Genesis, in canonical model foot order, scaled by `obs_scales.grf` (0.01). The decoder has 32 context inputs plus 12 torque inputs, hidden widths [128, 256, 512], and 12 force outputs. All three stages multiply physical torque by `policy.grf_torque_observation_scale` (0.01); ABL3's existing critic torque-observation scale remains unchanged.

The action queue runs before simulation. The final substep's actual queued action drives PD at that substep's starting joint position and velocity. Storage retains its actuator torque after Genesis force-range clipping, the affine PD coefficients and offsets, force limits, final-substep joint acceleration, a current-command causality flag, and the action clipping limit (86 values). Coefficients include randomized gains and channel weights. PACT includes motor strength; the existing ABL3 simulator does not multiply by motor strength, and this behavior is preserved.

Supervised reconstruction conditions on the stored, detached applied torque, even for delayed actions. Encoder and GRF decoder learn through separate supervised forwards; actor output heads have no path from this loss. The general privileged decoder excludes indices [61:73]; the critic still receives all observations. MSE remains the default. Huber delta is in scaled force units, ordinary MSE is logged separately, and PACT's prediction gate always uses MSE.

PACT's actor PINN forward replays the current differentiable command through action clipping and the cached final-substep PD law, then force clipping. Physical torque stays in Nm in the residual. Its normalized torque also enters the trainable GRF decoder without detachment. Endpoint mass, bias, and contact Jacobian are paired with backward velocity differences across that same final substep. These cached tensors are detached. The existing measured-contact fallback and predicted-force quality gate remain in place. Neither delays nor cached actions are bypassed: positive-delay transitions have no current-command PINN objective because the current command has not caused this transition. They remain supervised GRF examples. This deliberately does not assign a future-transition PINN loss back to an earlier queued command.

All reset-crossing examples, including timeouts and out-of-bounds resets, are excluded from reconstruction and PINN using rollout dones. This avoids mixing post-reset labels/physics with pre-reset actions; non-reset contact impacts remain valid. Masked losses divide by valid sample counts and return differentiable zero for an empty mask. Existing reward dynamics, PPO objectives, and curricula retain their original timing and behavior.

ABL3 checkpoint migration warns, removes the old GRF output rows from the privileged decoder, initializes the new GRF head, and leaves decoder optimizer moments fresh. New checkpoints save both decoder weights and optimizer states. PACT retains its existing shape-compatible legacy migration and reports missing legacy GRF heads.

Validation uses the actual queue and simulator step/PD methods with a deterministic fake robot, plus CPU PPO/auxiliary backward updates for both methods and both reconstruction modes. This is not a full Genesis rollout or training run. The residual remains an endpoint, finite-difference dynamics approximation; it does not differentiate simulated state trajectories, and model mismatch or unmodeled non-foot contacts can still affect it.

PACT-Pos pretraining uses the same detached applied-torque supervision, MSE/Huber modes, reset/timeout masks, and decoder normalization. Its final-substep actuator law is motor-strength-scaled PD, with no feedforward or channel weighting. Its existing PPO and torque-cloning objectives remain unchanged; there is no PINN loss or actor-head gradient from GRF reconstruction. The Go1 configuration opts into alignment; Go2 PACT-Pos retains its existing path. Legacy PACT-Pos checkpoints explicitly warn when the GRF head is missing, restore compatible privileged-decoder trunk weights, and leave resized output/head weights and decoder optimizer moments fresh. Modern decoder weights and optimizer moments round-trip through save/load.

PACT-Pos validation also covers its actual queue/PD/step methods, actor-head isolation, decoder dimensions, legacy and modern checkpoints, and CPU backward updates in both reconstruction modes. CPU tests disable its existing CUDA timing synchronizations only; the PPO, torque-cloning, and auxiliary losses and optimizers execute normally.

Payload observation normalization: all three Go1 stages multiply mass offset by `normalization.obs_scales.mass_offset = 0.125` and each CoM offset by `com_offset = 5.0`. Thus 8 kg and 0.20 m map to 1.0, while zero and signs are preserved. Fixed scales do not vary with the curriculum; PACT-Pos's smaller randomization range uses the same units as subsequent training. Scaling occurs in environment observation assembly, for both privileged critic/reconstruction inputs and the duplicate explicit encoder labels. Simulator parameters, dynamics, and rewards retain physical units. The mass squared-error contribution is reduced by a factor of 64; this balances units but does not establish that physical mass prediction accuracy has improved.

Existing checkpoints trained with raw payload labels have different critic/encoder/decoder units. No automatic weight conversion is performed. Use fresh training for the new normalization, or set both payload scales to 1.0 consistently across the three stages to retain the legacy observation convention. Physical-unit diagnostic errors can be recovered by dividing predictions and targets by their configured scales before comparison.
